Beyond the website, this edition is published in three forms. The data, free; the e-book and the programmatic interface, for a modest price.

The data. The complete text is available as a bulk download or on Hugging Face, under the terms of its Wikisource source. Each of the 36,691 articles is one record. Its text is in Markdown, with metadata, sections, and cross-references. The download includes three indexes that no other version of the 1911 Britannica provides: the cross-reference graph, joining every article to those it names; the topic index, classifying more than 90% of the articles by subject; and the contributor graph, resolving each author's initials to names and credentials. The topic index is faithfully reproduced from the original; the other two are available only here. The plain text can of course be had anywhere.

The book. The entire encyclopedia is available as a single e-book: all 36,691 articles and the more than 10,000 illustrations in one DRM-free EPUB, with maximum fidelity to the original. It is fully cross-linked, can be browsed by topic and by volume, and includes instant search of the corpus, full-text and by title, with no connection required. $39.99; corrected editions free to purchasers. A Kindle edition will be ready shortly. Volume 1 is free.

The interface. A programmatic interface, which will include full-text search, article retrieval, and traversal of the topics, contributors, and cross-references, is in preparation.

The maps. The edition's full-color fold-out maps — the celebrated Perthes maps among them — are collected in a separate maps bundle, free of charge: the plates as printed in 1911 and, where we have them, the Stieler Hand-Atlas originals they were engraved from, at full resolution. The same maps can be browsed here.
